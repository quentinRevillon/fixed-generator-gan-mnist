{\Large\bfseries Tuning the data, training and inference of a fixed-architecture GAN on MNIST\par}
\vspace{0.3cm}
\begin{minipage}{0.86\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Abstract.}
We train a GAN to generate handwritten digits (MNIST). The architecture of the generator is imposed, so we can only act on
three things: the training data, the way the model is trained, and the way images are drawn from it at the end. We test
each change alone, against the same baseline, with three random seeds and the score used by the course platform (the
Inception FID, lower is better). Only the training changes pay off: averaging the generator's weights over time (EMA) helps
on its own, and a ``relativistic'' loss with a gradient penalty helps on top of it. Together they bring the score from 38.7
to 22.1. Keeping only the most typical training images, or blurring the discriminator's view with noise, did not help.
Filtering generated images with the discriminator helps the baseline, but not the final model, whose discriminator can no
longer tell real from fake; retraining its last layer brings the gain back (22.0 to 19.7). Our submitted model combines
the new training with this filter and scores 19.4 on the MNIST test set. We also explain why we trust the Inception FID over a FID built on a digit classifier: the latter mostly
measures whether every digit is drawn equally often.
\end{minipage}
